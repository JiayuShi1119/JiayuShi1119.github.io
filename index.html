About Me

Engineering where hardware, firmware, and materials meet

A short story of how a curiosity about moving matter turned into a career in circuits.

My path into Electrical Engineering began with a simple question: how do physical materials and electronic signals work together to create intelligent motion? I started my undergraduate studies in Materials Science and Engineering at the Sichuan University–Pittsburgh Institute (SCUPI) before moving into Electrical and Computer Engineering, where I earned a 3.82/4.0 GPA. That cross-disciplinary start shapes how I think today. I do not see a circuit as an isolated schematic; I see it as the nervous system that brings a mechanism, a sensor, or a functional material to life.

I am happiest when I solve problems from first principles. I have written register-level ARM Cortex-M4 assembly without a hardware abstraction layer, laid out multi-layer PCBs in KiCad, and rebuilt the motion-control hardware of a 3D printer so that it could extrude experimental inks. During my internship at Shenzhen Bestsoon Electronic Technology, I learned how precision semiconductor equipment such as the ASMPT AD8312PIUS die bonder is operated, calibrated, and maintained on a real production floor. In Fall 2026 I transferred to Georgia Tech, where I joined the RoboJackets RoboCup Electrical Team and continue to grow as a hardware designer.

I also believe that great engineering depends on clear communication and trust. As President of the SCUPI Student Council, I led a 55-member organization, managed a $12,000 annual budget, and turned 313 pieces of student feedback into concrete campus improvements. Away from the workbench, you will find me studying the acoustics of handmade guitars or riding long gravel routes outside Atlanta. Each of these pursuits reminds me that careful measurement, patience, and good teamwork lead to the best results.

★ Discovery Project Feature

Custom Dual-Extruder DIW 3D Printer for Photo-Responsive Jumping Soft Robots

Role Project Leader Team 5 students · ECE, MSE, ME, IE Advisor Prof. Wenwen Xu Timeline Mar 2025 – Present
0.0008″Nozzle-to-bed height variance (0.02 mm)
10×Jump height vs. body length
820Thrust-to-weight ratio

01The Problem

Light-driven soft actuators can move without wires or batteries, which makes them attractive for micro-robots and for tight spaces such as the inside of the human body. Unfortunately, most photo-responsive materials, including liquid crystal elastomers, hydrogels, and photomechanical crystals, deform slowly, deliver little power, and are usually cast as fragile flat films. To make a soft robot that can actually jump, we needed a single three-dimensional part that combines a rigid base, a flexible hinge, a light-sensitive layer, and a latch-mediated spring mechanism that stores energy and releases it in milliseconds through a snap-through instability.

The bottleneck was manufacturing. No commercial 3D printer we could find was able to print volatile, solvent-based photo-responsive inks while also blending two materials continuously, so that stiff and soft regions would join through a smooth concentration gradient instead of peeling apart at a sharp interface.

02The Process

As project leader of a team spanning electrical, materials, mechanical, and industrial engineering, I organized our work as a closed loop of material, equipment, software, and structure:

  1. Mechatronic hardware. Starting from an open-source frame, I built a dual-extruder Direct Ink Writing (DIW) printer on a BigTreeTech Octopus Pro controller with TMC2209 stepper drivers, keeping the total hardware cost to roughly $240. To control solvent evaporation and warping, I added a hollow aluminum build plate with a heating element and a platinum RTD temperature sensor. I also built a precision leveling platform that held nozzle-to-bed variance within 0.0008 in (0.02 mm), and moved the power supply and mainboard to lower the center of gravity and stop the frame from flexing under the heavy syringe motors.
  2. Gradient slicer software. Standard slicers cannot mix two syringes, so we wrote the DIW G-code Gradient Converter. It reads ordinary single-nozzle G-code and rewrites it for two synchronized extruders, splitting each extrusion move as E = E1 + E2 according to a user-defined gradient formula. We wrapped it in a desktop GUI (Fig. 3) that previews the gradient field, calibrates the lag volume between the two syringes, and streams G-code to the printer over a serial port while staying compatible with Cura and Pronterface, so slicing, previewing, and printing happen in one workflow.
  3. Ink formulation and tuning. With my materials teammates, I helped formulate inks containing azobenzene chromophores, PVP, and fumed silica. We used meniscus-guided crystallization in binary solvents (for example, 8:2 DMF:ethanol at 194–212 °F, or 90–100 °C) and swept print speeds from F2.2 to F5.0 until crystal growth kept pace with solidification.

03The Outcome

The upgraded printer reached an alignment precision of 0.004 in (0.1 mm) and printed continuous concentration gradients across four distinct transition zones. Using the tuned parameters, we produced lightweight latch-based actuators that jump repeatedly under UV light, reaching an unloaded jump height of 10 times their body length, a thrust-to-weight ratio of 820, and a work density of about 40,000 J/m³.

Beyond the numbers, this project taught me how to lead people from different disciplines toward a shared technical goal, and how electrical design choices such as sensor placement, driver selection, and power layout directly limit what a materials scientist can achieve. The work is now being prepared as a software copyright for the gradient G-code generator, an invention patent for the modified printer, and a journal manuscript.

Daisy-Chained Multi-Sensor PCB

RoboJackets RoboCup · Electrical Hardware Designer · Fall 2026 – Present

A 4-layer, 3.15 × 1.97 in (80 × 50 mm) RP2040 board that collects data from six I²C sensors and passes a growing data packet to the next board over SPI.

KiCadRP20404-Layer PCBI²C / SPIPower OR-ing
Problem · Process · Outcome

01The Problem

Autonomous soccer robots need compact, noise-resistant sensor suites that monitor motion, orientation, temperature, magnetic fields, and color without a tangle of wiring. The board also had to accept power from either USB or the previous board in the chain without ever back-feeding either source.

02The Process

I captured a hierarchical schematic in KiCad around an RP2040 with external flash, then integrated an ICM-42605 IMU, LM75 temperature sensor, TMAG5273 Hall-effect sensor, TCA9534 GPIO expander with a DIP switch, VEML3328 color sensor, and an IS31FL3254 driver for eight RGB LEDs. An LM66100 ideal-diode controller selects between USB and chain power before an AP2112K 3.3 V regulator. On the 4-layer stack-up I dedicated internal 3.3 V and ground planes and worked through tight QFN fan-out and clearance rules.

03The Outcome

The schematic, layout, and design rule check are complete and the board is in fabrication. Next, I will bring up the firmware with the Pico SDK and validate every I²C device before the competition season.

STM32L476 Bare-Metal Embedded Systems

Project Leader · Advisor: Prof. Fashu Xu · Mar – Jul 2026

Deterministic, register-level firmware in C and ARM Cortex-M4 assembly for keypads, stepper motors, interrupts, and I²C sensors.

CARM AssemblySTM32L476Keil µVision 5Oscilloscope
Problem · Process · Outcome

01The Problem

Vendor hardware abstraction layers make development faster, but they add overhead and hide the timing details that matter in real-time control. Our goal was to understand and control the microcontroller directly, one register at a time.

02The Process

I led the firmware architecture in Keil µVision 5, writing mixed C and assembly routines that configure the clock tree, GPIO registers, and NVIC/EXTI interrupts. On top of these drivers we built a matrix keypad scanner, stepper motor phase sequencing, and an I²C sensor interface, and we verified signal timing and bus integrity with a digital storage oscilloscope.

03The Outcome

We delivered a responsive control system with reliable multi-peripheral I²C communication and predictable interrupt latency. The experience gave me the low-level confidence I now use when planning firmware bring-up for my RoboCup board.

Digital Logic Breadboard Prototyping

Digital Circuits & Systems Lab · SCUPI

Hands-on design, wiring, and debugging of combinational and sequential logic circuits built from discrete logic ICs.

Logic ICsBoolean DesignBreadboardingDebugging
Problem · Process · Outcome

01The Problem

Logic that works on paper often fails on the bench because of wiring mistakes, floating inputs, or timing issues. The lab challenge was to turn truth tables and state diagrams into working hardware.

02The Process

I simplified each design with Boolean algebra and Karnaugh maps, mapped it onto standard logic ICs, and wired it on a breadboard with DIP switches and push buttons as inputs and LEDs as outputs. I labeled key nodes, tested one block at a time, and traced faults with a logic probe and multimeter.

03The Outcome

All circuits met their truth tables and state sequences, and I developed a systematic debugging habit: isolate, measure, and then fix, which I still use on every board I build.

Guitar & Lutherie Acoustics

I am a fingerstyle and acoustic guitarist and have performed live at the Xiancao Music Festival. What fascinates me most is the physics inside the instrument: how tonewoods such as Adirondack spruce, rosewood, and mahogany, together with bracing patterns like scalloped X-bracing and V-Class bracing, shape resonance and frequency response.

To me, a guitar top is a beautifully tuned mechanical filter—the same ideas I study in signals and systems, carved in wood.

Endurance Road & Gravel Cycling

I founded and captained a 14-member collegiate cycling team, organized the Longquan Mountain Challenge, and completed a 310-mile (500 km) endurance tour around Hainan Island. Today I explore Atlanta’s road and gravel routes on a 700c × 40c gravel bike.

Long rides teach pacing, preparation, and calm problem solving when something breaks far from home.

Travel & Exploration

Travel is how I recharge. I have hiked the river valleys and canola fields of Qinghai’s Hainan Tibetan Autonomous Prefecture, fed seagulls on Dianchi Lake in Kunming, kayaked the lake below Mount Wawu in Sichuan, and watched the sun set over the sea on Pingtan Island in Fujian.
